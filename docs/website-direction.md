# WeAIForge website direction

## Purpose and audience
Help founders and business teams recognise a manual workflow worth improving and start a scoped conversation. WeAIForge is presented as a focused AI engineering studio.

## Primary offer
Start with one workflow: discovery, a working prototype using agreed sample data, feasibility findings, and a separately scoped production proposal. Timeline and fee are agreed after an initial conversation; no invented price or delivery guarantee.

## Homepage sequence
1. Operations-focused positioning, a concrete customer-request problem, and primary contact links.
2. The starting engagement: deliverables, inputs, initial conversation, written scope and separate rollout decision.
3. One interactive operations example with clear stage states and explicit approval.
4. A business-led Jev section with a theme-aware workflow illustration and accurate proposed-integration disclosure.
5. A factual implementation breakdown of the self-directed prototype, with limitations.
6. Delivery practices, secondary embedded engineering, FAQs and contact.

## Visual decisions
Preserve the logo, font families, accent palettes, and Light/Dark/System preferences. Use responsive gutters, left-aligned text, readable product content, and restrained section changes. Avoid slogan-heavy copy, decorative numbering, repeated arrow motifs, and multiple competing demonstrations. The screenshot slideshow, three-example dashboard, and equal-prominence service cards are retired from the active page.

## Evidence and claims
The demonstration uses fictional data, is browser-only, and sends nothing. Do not imply a live model, external integration, customer deployment, measured business result, or named expertise without evidence. Explain engineering decisions through the demo and concrete engagement terms.

## Service pages
Keep existing URLs. The workflow-development page adds production scope: integrations, quality checks, action boundaries, and operation. The embedded-engineering page adds working arrangements, allocation, milestones, and handover. Both retain the common contact and starting offer.

## Preferences and accessibility
System appearance follows the OS unless explicitly overridden; accent is saved independently. Keep keyboard operation, visible focus, human approval, meaningful form labels, required-field validation, and responsive mobile stacking. Calendly loads on request; email enquiries open a draft for the visitor to send.

## Jev decision demonstration
One compact workspace shows a customer request beside its proposed action, with a native scenario selector and subdued, clickable stage navigation. It opens at the draft stage so visitors see the outcome immediately. The three fictional scenarios are delayed delivery, missing tracking information, and refund requests. Each has a predefined route, policy, and draft; changing the scenario clears edits and approval.

A short decision strip states the chosen route. Supporting records, policy and the Jev explanation are collapsed under “Why this decision?”. There is one primary action at a time: inspect records, view the proposal, review, approve, or try again. Editing clears approval; empty actions cannot proceed. Completion requires explicit approval and performs no external action.

The disclosure identifies a simulated concept with fictional data. Jev is not connected, and decisions and drafts are predefined. Do not add invented probabilities, performance results or partnership claims. A live integration would require server-side credentials, evaluation and enforced approval boundaries.

## Jev visual section
A dedicated section above the demo makes Jev visible through an original SVG workflow graphic, not a vendor screenshot or implied live product. The visual uses the saved accent and light/dark tokens. Desktop uses a horizontal decision tree; mobile uses a separate vertical composition so labels stay readable. Text and figure align at their top edge, and all main sections share page gutters. The demo heading columns align at their top edge as well. Attribution links to TypeSafe AI, and the copy clearly identifies the illustrated architecture and simulated demo.

## Buyer-first refinement
The first engagement appears before the technology explanation. No invented price, timeframe or project history is stated. The prototype breakdown describes implemented browser behaviour, not AI quality or production experience. It cannot replace a genuine deployment case study; future project evidence must come from actual work.

Default contact options are scheduling and direct email. A direct-send form and Vercel email endpoint are prepared but disabled until hosting environment settings, provider credentials, rate limits and privacy disclosures are configured. The UI never claims an enquiry was sent after merely opening an email application. See README for setup and mocked tests.
